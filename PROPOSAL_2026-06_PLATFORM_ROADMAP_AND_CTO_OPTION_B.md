# OPTION B

# Re-New Platform: State, Roadmap, and a Proposal

## 1. WAVE is already the operating system of Re-New

WAVE started with a practical need: one place where Re-New could see the activity. The website gave the project a public front door, the campaign tool cleaned up intake, and since January WAVE has become the relationship system behind the business. Roughly 3,000 historical records now sit in one source of truth. The first clean operating month counted 56 new repreneurs without manual reconciliation, so product market fit can be reviewed from live data instead of memory.

The bottleneck has moved. The entrepreneur side is in WAVE. Opportunities and M&A firms still sit across inboxes, Excel files and judgement calls. With 17 paying customers, 120 live opportunities and around 400 intermediaries, the next problem is matching: which repreneur should see which opportunity, under what confidentiality rules, and with what follow up.

The next step is to bring that same discipline to opportunities. The operating flow should stay controlled: opportunities enter once, intermediary constraints are respected, NDA steps are traceable, and each match records why it was proposed, what happened next and whether it created revenue.

Helsinki is a good example. You saw the fit by memory, three days before it became obvious, and it helped one client. WAVE should make that same move possible across every opportunity and every paying repreneur, while keeping the final judgement human. That is the platform I think Re-New needs, and it is a direction I can own without waiting for a feature list.

## 2. The resourcing question

A junior developer at 2,200 euro per month gives you hands without direction. They need days to learn the codebase and weeks to really own the business dynamics. Most likely, you still have to play the CTO above them and decide what to build.

I already built the junior: the Notion intake you are filling right now feeds an AI pipeline that ingests requests, executes, and reports back. The missing piece is the person who designs and runs that system, and who wrote every line of the platform.

## 3. What I propose

Two documents, one structure, within the budget you had:

- **Services.** Re-New contracts PUSHAPP S.R.L. for product and platform development: 1,500 euro per month, 10 hours per week, monthly invoicing, 30 days notice either way. Around 300 to 500 euro per month of the remaining budget goes to AI tooling, which is the dev. Total: 1,800 to 2,000 euro per month, inside the 2,200 you had in budget. Everything built under this contract is assigned to Re-New as each invoice is paid.
- **Upside.** My rate for this role would be 3,000 euro per month: I bill half and invest the other half in Re-New, for up to 8 percent equity* on today's fully diluted capital, vesting monthly from July to December if we pass the September 30 review. It converts only if a qualified financing happens: at least 100,000 euro of new outside money, friends and family top ups excluded. If the raise comes within 18 months after the term, the vested part still converts; if it never comes, it lapses and we shake hands as friends.
- **Term.** July 1 to December 31, with a no fault review on September 30. If either of us is not happy at that point, we stop cleanly: Re-New keeps the work already paid for, I keep the invoices already paid, and the equity side lapses entirely. No drama, no debt, no awkward tail. If we both decide to continue after September 30, the equity keeps vesting monthly until December 31 under the same qualified financing condition.

This structure keeps cash below the junior budget, and the rest of my compensation exists only if we make Re-New fundable together.

## 4. First 90 days

The first 90 days should stay narrow. July closes the testing loop and the bug backlog, with access, hosting, repository ownership and database control hardened. August moves the opportunity side into WAVE, with ingestion, structured fields and the first matching loop against the repreneur base. September tests AI assisted matching on real deal flow, adds KPI v2, and gives us a clear September 30 checkpoint.

We should keep the exact backlog flexible, because a startup will change priorities week by week. What changes compared with the ICP is the delivery model: you should expect around 5x the operational delivery bandwidth, with more autonomy and proactivity from my side. I identify useful moves, propose them, test them and bring you decisions instead of waiting for feature by feature direction.

---

\* **Why up to 8 percent.** I am sharing openly the reasoning behind the number so you can see what I see and we can discuss the rationale, not just the percentage. First, the cash at risk is 1,500 euro per month of deferred rate over six months, so 9,000 euro that converts only if Re-New raises qualified outside money. At a realistic 40 percent probability of that event, the fair price of the claim is about 22,500 euro. An 8 percent claim at 22,500 euro implies a company value around 280,000 euro. I am not trying to value Re-New here. I am pricing the risk of this specific claim. Second, advisory or fractional CTOs often sit around 1 to 4 percent for guidance, while founding or full time CTO roles are closer to 15 to 25 percent. Six months of founder grade execution at half cash rate sits between those two. Third, if no qualified raise happens, the equity expires worthless and Re-New's cost was a CTO level operator at 1,500 euro per month.
