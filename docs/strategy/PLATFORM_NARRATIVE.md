# A Vision for Re-New's Next Sprint



## Where we are today

The platform is live. It works. It replaced Flatchr. The team can manage repreneurs, track their journey, score their readiness, and send personalized emails. We went from zero to a functioning CRM in about eight weeks, and the foundation is solid.

But let's be honest about what it is right now: it's an internal tool.

Repreneurs fill a questionnaire. They receive a thank you email. And then they wait. They have no visibility into their own profile. They can't see their scores, their journey stage, or what milestones they've hit.

The thing is, what sits behind the admin dashboard is genuinely sophisticated. And I think that's worth looking at closely, because it's the basis for everything I'm about to suggest.

---

## What Re-New already has (and why it matters)

This is the part I want to spend time on, because from where I sit, Re-New has built something that I haven't seen anywhere else in the ecosystem. And I don't think the team fully realizes how far ahead this puts you.

### Process mastery encoded in software

The 3-tier assessment system isn't just scoring. It's a structured framework that:

- Separates profile quality (WHO: 0-100) from project maturity (WHEN: 0-100)
- Uses financial triangulation to detect incoherence (ambition vs. means)
- Flags logical contradictions (5 warning flags that override scores)
- Maps 11 readiness milestones across 4 journey stages
- Generates automatic recommendations (deal_flow / priority_interview / interview / starter_pack)

No training program, no deal platform, no government agency does this. Bertrand's methodology isn't just expertise, it's become a system. That's rare, and it's exactly the kind of thing that scales.

### The dual scoring innovation

Traditional M&A qualification asks one question: "is this buyer good?" The platform asks two separate questions:

- **WHO**: Can this person execute? (experience, leadership, crisis management, investment decisions)
- **WHEN**: Is the project viable? (deal size vs. equity vs. structure coherence)

A brilliant operator with no financial plan gets different guidance than a well-funded novice. This nuance is, in my opinion, Re-New's core intellectual property. It reflects years of Bertrand's pattern recognition, now structured in a way that can work at scale.

### The journey-centric data model

The platform already tracks repreneurs across:

- **Lifecycle stages**: lead / qualified / client
- **Journey stages**: explorer / learner / ready / serial_acquirer
- **11 binary milestones** across 4 journey categories

This isn't just a CRM. It's the foundation for something more.

### What's missing

Here's what I think is the gap:

- No repreneur-facing portal (repreneurs can't see their own data)
- No content library (knowledge is in Bertrand's head, not yet on the platform)
- No payment processing
- No certification display or verification system
- No seller/intermediary facing profiles

The intelligence exists. The repreneurs just can't see it. And I think that's the highest leverage thing to change.

---

## A suggestion: make the methodology visible

The idea I'd like to put on the table is straightforward: take what we already have and make it visible to the repreneurs themselves.

Right now, Re-New's process mastery lives behind the admin dashboard. What if the methodology was the product? What if the structured journey, the scoring, the milestones, the stages, became something repreneurs could see, interact with, and use to actually get better at buying a business?

The shift, in one sentence: from internal CRM to repreneur-facing acceleration platform.

I see two reasons why this could be worth exploring.

**For repreneurs, it would create real value.** You know better than I do that most aspiring buyers have no map. A platform that shows them exactly where they stand, what gaps they have, and what to do next, that's something I haven't found anywhere in the market. It gives them clarity, motivation, and a credible path forward.

**For sellers and intermediaries, it could create trust.** When a seller meets a potential buyer, they have one question: "Is this person serious?" Today, there's no structured way to answer that. But imagine a Re-New certified repreneur comes with a readiness profile showing verified financing, documented industry expertise, completed preparation milestones. The buyer's credibility becomes visible at a glance. That could change the dynamics of every deal Re-New touches.

### How it could work

Here's one way to think about the flow. This is a suggestion, not a blueprint:

```
1. INTAKE (public)
   Repreneur fills questionnaire
   → automatic WHO/WHEN scoring + warning flags

2. GATE (team decision)
   Minimum score threshold
   → accepted or redirected to resources

3. ONBOARDING (the moment of value)
   Personalized intelligence message showing Re-New "gets it"
   → "Based on your profile, you're at [stage]. Your strengths: [X,Y].
      Your gaps: [A,B]. Here's what to focus on next."

4. PORTAL (repreneur-facing)
   Journey map showing position and progress
   Competence radar chart (6-dimension readiness)
   Milestone tracking with certification badges
   Resource library (gated by tier)

5. DEVELOPMENT (ongoing)
   Self-paced resources (free tier)
   Coaching sessions (paid)
   Certification validations (automated + human)

6. DEAL FLOW (for qualified repreneurs)
   Access to acquisition opportunities
   Re-New facilitation + commission on closed deals
```

## What this looks like in practice: four moments

Strategy documents are abstract. Let me show you four moments instead. These are fictional repreneurs, but the situations are drawn from what I've seen in the data. Each one illustrates a different part of how the platform could create value.

### Moment 1: The onboarding message

Karim, 45, operations director in logistics. He submits Re-New's intake questionnaire. He expects "thanks, we'll be in touch." Twenty minutes later, an email arrives:

> *"Merci Karim. You bring 18 years of leadership experience including crisis management and investment decisions. That's exactly the kind of execution capability that makes acquisitions succeed.*
>
> *Your project is at the exploratory stage, targeting 1-2M deals in industrial services. Your financial structure has some gaps we should discuss: your equity target of 150K against a 1.5M deal size suggests a financing ratio that most banks will question.*
>
> *Based on your profile, you're in the Explorer phase. Here's what successful repreneurs at your stage focus on next: building a realistic financing plan, defining a clear target profile, and assembling an advisory team.*
>
> *While our team reviews your profile, here are resources to get started: [links]*"

**What Karim feels**: "They actually read my answers. They spotted the financing gap I was worried about. These people know what they're doing."

**Revenue moment**: None yet. Free. But Karim went from "another form" to "these people understand my situation." When he gets portal access, he logs in immediately.

**What this demonstrates**: Re-New's process mastery creates trust before any human interaction. Every competitor sends a generic confirmation. Re-New sends insight. The questionnaire isn't data collection for Re-New's benefit. It's the beginning of a structured relationship where the repreneur gets something back.

### Moment 2: First login

Sophie, 42, ex-COO of a manufacturing company. She had her Re-New interview and just received portal access.

She logs in to see: her **readiness radar** (Financial Capacity 7, Industry Expertise 8, Deal Experience 2, Operational Capability 9, Network Strength 3, Process Maturity 2); her **journey map** (13 phases, two green with badges, the rest visible but grey); and **recommended next actions** ("Your biggest gap is Deal Experience at 2/9. Here are 3 things to move it to 4/9").

**What Sophie feels**: "I'm not starting from zero. I can see exactly what I need to do." For the first time, someone gave her a map instead of telling her to "just start looking."

**Revenue moment**: Sophie clicks "Build Target Screening Scorecard" and hits the Pro paywall. She subscribes at 149/month. Three minutes on the platform.

**What this demonstrates**: Making the invisible visible is its own value proposition. Sophie already had strengths and gaps. She just couldn't see them. The free tier shows what she needs. The Pro tier gives tools to act on it. Conversion happens naturally.

### Moment 3: Earning a certification

Marc, 38, Pro subscriber for 4 months. He's evaluating 2 targets and wants the "Evaluation Competent" badge for his shareable profile. Two options: an **online assessment** (included in Pro, 45 minute case study with anonymized financials) or a **supervised evaluation** (800 EUR consulting session on his real target, with a Re-New advisor).

Marc picks Option B. Real target, real coaching, real certification. Two birds, one stone.

**What Marc feels**: "This isn't a quiz. I'm learning something I need right now, on my real deal." The badge isn't a participation trophy. He earned it.

**Revenue moment**: 800 EUR consulting + ongoing Pro subscription. The evaluation also reveals Marc needs help structuring his LOI. He books a follow-up LDC Review (1,000 EUR). One certification triggers 1,800 EUR.

**What this demonstrates**: Certifications could generate revenue by being genuinely useful, not mandatory. The journey surfaces needs, the platform connects them to solutions. Re-New wouldn't need to sell services. It would surface the right service at the right moment.

### Moment 4: The seller's perspective

Jean-Pierre, 61, owns a plumbing services company (15 employees, 1.8M revenue). His intermediary shows him two buyer candidates.

**Candidate A** (another platform): "Interested buyer, 15 years experience, has financing." One paragraph.

**Candidate B** (Re-New certified): Readiness radar showing Financial Capacity 8/9 (bank pre-approval verified), Industry Expertise 6/9 (10 years in services), Operational Capability 8/9 (managed 40 person teams), Process Maturity 7/9 (9 certifications, LDC validated on a previous target). Plus: "Completed DD preparation course and negotiation workshop."

Jean-Pierre asks to meet Candidate B first.

**What Jean-Pierre feels**: "I can trust this person won't waste my time. They've done the work."

**Revenue moment**: Deal closes at 1.8M. Re-New earns 2% commission = 36,000 EUR. The repreneur's journey already generated ~12,000 in subscription and consulting over 10 months. Total lifetime value: ~48,000 EUR.

**What this demonstrates**: A certification system could close the trust gap that kills deals. The readiness radar answers in one glance the question that normally takes three meetings: "Is this buyer real?" Re-New wouldn't charge for matchmaking. Re-New would charge because its preparation process produces buyers who actually close.

## The building blocks

Behind the four moments above, there's a structured framework. Let me outline the pieces and how they connect to what already exists.

### A 13 phase acquisition lifecycle

Based on research across ETA frameworks (Stanford, IESE, HBS), French market specifics (CRA, CCI, BPI), and M&A best practices, I've mapped a scaffold of the full buyer's journey. Each phase has defined deliverables, a certification, and a set of resources and tools that Re-New could create over time.

| Phase | Name | Certification | Verification | Timeline |
|-------|------|---------------|-------------|----------|
| 0 | Ideation & Exploration | Explorer | Auto (questionnaire done) | 1-3 months |
| 1 | Self-Assessment | Profile Verified | Human (Re-New interview) | 1-2 months |
| 2 | Acquisition Thesis | Thesis Validated | Human (Re-New review) | 1-2 months |
| 3 | Financial Preparation | Financially Verified | Document (proof of funds) | 2-3 months |
| 4 | Advisory Team | Team Ready | Self-declared + verified | 1-2 months |
| 5 | Deal Sourcing | Active Searcher | Activity-based | 3-6 months |
| 6 | Target Evaluation | Evaluation Competent | Assessment/practice | 2-4 weeks/target |
| 7 | Letter of Intent | LDC Validated | Document (Re-New review) | 2-4 weeks |
| 8 | Due Diligence | DD Competent | Course + assessment | 45-60 days |
| 9 | Negotiation | Negotiation Ready | Workshop or experience | 2-4 weeks |
| 10 | Financing | Financing Secured | Document (commitments) | 2-4 weeks |
| 11 | Closing | Deal Closed | Verified acquisition | 1-2 weeks |
| 12 | Transition | Transition Complete | Re-New confirms | 3-12 months |
| 13 | First 100 Days | Certified Repreneur | Full journey | ~3 months |

14 certifications total. Each a verified achievement, not gamification. Each answers a question a seller would actually ask. And each builds on the previous, creating a visible progression from Explorer to Certified Repreneur.

The platform already handles Phases 0, 1, and 7 partially (questionnaire, WHO/WHEN scoring, LDC milestone tracking). The rest is greenfield, to be built incrementally.

### The 6-dimension readiness radar

Instead of a single score, this model shows a multi-dimensional readiness profile:

| Dimension | What It Measures | Data Sources (already in platform) |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Financial Capacity** (1-9) | Proof of funds, financing structure, post-close liquidity | WHEN score, milestone certs |
| **Industry Expertise** (1-9) | Years in target sectors, C-level experience, domain knowledge | WHO score, Tier 2 interview |
| **Deal Experience** (1-9) | Prior acquisitions, advisory background, training completed | WHO score, Tier 2 interview |
| **Operational Capability** (1-9) | Team management, crisis handling, change leadership | WHO score, Tier 2 interview |
| **Network Strength** (1-9) | Advisors retained, board, industry connections | Team Ready cert, Tier 2 interview |
| **Process Maturity** (1-9) | Milestones completed, certifications earned, timeline adherence | Tier 3 milestones, cert count |

Here's what I find elegant about this: it's not a new assessment. It's a new visualization of data Re-New already collects. The WHO score feeds Industry Expertise and Operational Capability. The WHEN score feeds Financial Capacity and Process Maturity. The Tier 2 interview fills in the competence dimensions. We'd be reorganizing existing data into a format that's useful to repreneurs and sellers alike.

The certification design principles would follow credible models (CFA, PMP, ICF):

- **Verified, not self-declared**: every certification requires evidence or automated verification
- **Achievement-based, not gamification**: badges represent real milestones
- **Useful to third parties**: each answers a question sellers actually ask
- **Progressive**: later certifications build on earlier ones
- **Revenue-aligned**: higher certifications naturally require engagement that generates revenue

### Revenue streams

Five streams, phased over time. I won't suggest specific pricing because that's a conversation for the team and needs validation with an initial cohort. But here's the structure I see:

1. **Platform subscriptions** (Free / Pro / Premium): recurring revenue. Free shows repreneurs their journey. Pro gives tools. Premium adds dedicated advisor access and priority deal flow.
2. **Coaching & consulting**: Re-New's existing strength, amplified. The journey surfaces needs automatically. The right service appears at the right moment.
3. **Deal commissions** (1-3% of deal value): the primary long term driver. Even conservatively, 5 deals/year at 2% on 2M average = 200K before subscriptions or coaching.
4. **Certification fees**: assessment and course fees at the moment repreneurs are most motivated.
5. **AI credits**: specialized tools (valuation analysis, DD question generator), usage-based, included in paid tiers.

## Why I think this could work

### For the team

- **Leverages what's built**: the 3-tier assessment, dual scoring, journey stages, and milestone tracking are in production. This would add a repreneur-facing layer on top, not replace anything.
- **Keeps consulting at the core**: the platform amplifies Bertrand's reach. It doesn't replace the human touch.
- **Multiple revenue streams**: not dependent on any single source.
- **Gradual rollout**: each piece delivers value independently. We wouldn't need to build everything to start.

### For repreneurs

- **Clarity**: they know exactly where they are and what to do next
- **Progress**: visible milestones and certifications create motivation
- **Credibility**: certifications make them more attractive to sellers
- **Resources**: structured content replaces scattered Google searches
- **Community**: they're not alone in the process

### For sellers and intermediaries

- **Trust**: Re-New certification would mean verified capability, not just expressed interest
- **Efficiency**: the readiness radar tells them instantly if a buyer is serious
- **Quality**: pre-qualified, coached, certified buyers close faster

## Brand Pyramid

As an exercise, I tried mapping where Re-New sits as a brand if this vision were realized. This is my reading of it. You'll know better than me whether it rings true.

**Attributes**: What Re-New *is*. A curated platform (not everyone gets in). Structured (methodology, not opinions). Verified (certifications require proof). Progressive (you earn your way up). Dual-sided (value flows to both buyers and sellers).

**Reasons to believe**: Why anyone should trust this. The assessment system already works. The dual scoring is unique. The lifecycle framework is researched and comprehensive. And behind it all, Bertrand's two decades of M&A pattern recognition, now encoded in a system that scales.

**Functional benefits**: What it does for people. It gives repreneurs a structured path with measurable milestones. It turns invisible readiness into something visible and verifiable. It gives sellers a way to tell real buyers from dreamers in one glance.

**Emotional benefits**: How it makes people feel. Repreneurs feel confident because they can see their progress. They feel clarity because someone finally gave them a map. They feel they belong to something serious, not just another mailing list. The core feeling: "I know exactly where I stand, and I know what to do next."

**Brand essence**: The one thing Re-New stands for if this vision comes together. *Readiness you can prove.* Not readiness you claim. Not readiness someone assumes. Readiness that's been measured, developed, verified, and made visible. That's the promise to repreneurs ("we'll make you provably ready") and to sellers ("our buyers are provably real").

### What this means practically

If the team agrees that "readiness you can prove" captures something true about Re-New's ambition, it becomes a filter for decisions. Every feature, every piece of content, every certification should make readiness more measurable, more developable, or more visible. If it doesn't do one of those three things, it probably doesn't belong in the first version.
