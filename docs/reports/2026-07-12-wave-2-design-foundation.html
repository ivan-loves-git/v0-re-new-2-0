Getting ready to sprint: Re-New UI / UX consolidation

Re-New is entering a new chapter. I used the window before the next wave of product requirements to consolidate the experience and give the platform a stronger foundation for what comes next.

01 · Complete UI / UX consolidation One coherent product experience
02 · Next chapter Security and cybersecurity A separate, focused body of work
03 · Ready Faster product delivery More focus on new value, less reinvention
Executive summary

A deliberate investment before acceleration

The next phase of Re-New will bring more workflows, more users and more product decisions. Before accelerating into that work, I wanted to make sure the current product could support it without becoming increasingly fragmented or difficult to evolve.

I rationalized the experience across dashboards, filters, tables, profiles, opportunities, mobile views and the repreneur journey. This is more than a visual refresh. Information is easier to scan, actions are easier to find, and new capabilities can be introduced faster without disrupting the experience people already know.

What this buys us
  • 01Faster delivery of the next product requirements
  • 02Less rework and fewer inconsistencies
  • 03Clearer daily decisions for the team
  • 04A product experience aligned with Re-New's next phase
Why now

Use the quiet window well

The space before new requirements was the right moment to improve the foundations without interrupting an active delivery sprint.

What changed

One product, not separate screens

Major staff workflows and repreneur touchpoints now share the same visual logic, interaction patterns and information priorities.

What follows

Build forward with confidence

Future work starts from an established foundation, so more of the available effort can go into useful new capabilities.

The change in practice

Six product changes, now governed by one visual language

The examples below show how the same consolidation improves daily staff work, decision quality and the external repreneur experience. The current-production evidence that follows shows how the visual foundation now keeps those gains coherent as the product grows.

All major product areas covered
01
From features to decisions

See the state of the business quickly

The dashboard now leads with business state, people requiring attention and the work that should happen next.

Before

Independent cards competed for attention and repeated navigation took up useful space.

Now

Core indicators, ranked profiles, review queues and trends follow a clear reading order.

Why it matters

Less interpretation before the team can decide where to focus.

02
Filters when needed, space when not

Filter complex lists without clutter

Search stays immediate. People add only the filters they need, see every active choice and remove it visually in one step.

Before

Seven permanent controls made every visit feel like a configuration task.

Now

An “Add filter” menu keeps options available while removable chips show what is active.

Why it matters

Less clutter, faster cohort building and a repeatable interaction across the product.

Applied consistently across Find, Groups, Pipeline, Opportunities and M&A
03
One visible path from lead to client

Move work through the pipeline

The lifecycle is now a calm working surface that makes volume, movement and stalled profiles easier to understand.

Before

Lifecycle information was available, but lists and controls varied between working areas.

Now

Search, filtering, sorting, status signals and profile cards follow one predictable pattern.

Why it matters

The team can move between stages without relearning how each screen works.

04
Every relationship has a next move

Know what to do for each repreneur

Each profile now starts with the person, their current situation and the next best action.

Before

Identity, scores, milestones and actions were fragmented across separate areas.

Now

Relationship context, qualification, readiness, opportunities and notes form one sequence.

Why it matters

Profiles support relationship management instead of simply storing information.

05
Deal risk and follow-up in the right order

Manage opportunities through priorities

The opportunity area now gives precedence to responses, active pursuits and freshness risks before the general inventory.

Before

The dashboard showed what existed, while immediate follow-up was distributed lower on the page.

Now

Response review and active pursuits appear first, with stage and document status visible.

Why it matters

Attention is directed towards the work that can move a deal forward or prevent it from stalling.

06
The same quality reaches repreneurs

Carry one experience across every context

The consolidation reaches beyond internal dashboards into intake, sign-in, mobile use and the external opportunity journey.

Before

Staff tools and repreneur touchpoints could feel like separate layers of the platform.

Now

Language, navigation, actions and information hierarchy remain familiar across audiences and screen sizes.

Why it matters

A coherent external experience reinforces trust at the moments repreneurs interact directly with Re-New.

07
A foundation that holds as we add

Make visual quality repeatable, not dependent on a redesign

The new visual pass is now part of the product foundation. New screens inherit a calmer hierarchy, consistent panels, compact labels, disciplined emphasis and one chart language rather than inventing a new style each time.

Before

A good new screen could still introduce its own visual habits, gradually making the product less coherent.

Now

Reusable visual rules make density, emphasis, metrics and filtering feel like parts of the same operating system.

Why it matters

The team can ship faster while keeping the product familiar, credible and easier to trust.
